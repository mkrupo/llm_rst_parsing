### Antithesis
- **N:** W regards N positively and identifies with it.
- **S:** W regards S as less important or rejects it.
- **N/S:** N and S are incompatible, and recognizing the contrast increases R's positive regard for N.
- **Example:** (SN-Antithesis (text "At one point they seemed to have succeeded.") (text "But the buyer did not pay."))

### Background
- **N/S:** Understanding S makes it easier for R to understand N by supplying relevant prior or general information.
- **Effect:** R's ability to comprehend N is increased.
- **Example:** (SN-Background (text "Until 1984, Burkina Faso was called Obervolta.") (text "Many Europeans believe they are two countries."))

### Circumstance
- **S:** S supplies a temporal, spatial, situational, or interpretive framework.
- **N/S:** N is interpreted within the framework in S.
- **Effect:** R recognizes the framework for interpreting N.
- **Example:** (SN-Circumstance (text "When the market opened,") (text "prices rose immediately."))

### Concession
- **S:** W acknowledges S although it is apparently incompatible with N.
- **N/S:** N still holds despite S and is more central.
- **Effect:** R's positive regard for N is increased.
- **Example:** (SN-Concession (text "Although facilities are normally standard,") (text "this campground lacks them."))

### Condition
- **S:** S is hypothetical, future, or unrealized.
- **N/S:** Realization of N depends on realization of S.
- **Example:** (SN-Condition (text "If temperatures fall,") (text "the road will freeze."))

### Elaboration
- **N/S:** S gives additional detail about N or an element of N, including set-member, abstraction-instance, whole-part, process-step, object-attribute, or general-specific detail.
- **Effect:** R recognizes the added detail.
- **Example:** (NS-Elaboration (text "The committee approved several measures.") (text "One measure funds new shelters."))

### Enablement
- **N:** N is an action to be performed by R.
- **N/S:** S increases R's potential ability to perform N.
- **Example:** (NS-Enablement (text "Replace the spark plugs.") (text "A wrench is under the cap."))

### Evaluation
- **N/S:** S expresses an evaluative assessment of N.
- **Effect:** R recognizes the assessment.
- **Example:** (NS-Evaluation (text "The court searched for a buyer for years.") (text "That failure was disastrous."))

### Evidence
- **N:** R may not already believe N strongly enough.
- **N/S:** S is intended to increase R's belief in N.
- **Example:** (NS-Evidence (text "The policy is ineffective.") (text "Independent audits found no measurable improvement."))

### Interpretation
- **N/S:** S interprets the significance of N or relates it to a framework of ideas not explicit in N.
- **Example:** (NS-Interpretation (text "They bought their first luxury car.") (text "That makes them part of the bourgeoisie."))

### Justify
- **N/S:** S increases R's readiness to accept W's right, basis, or authority to present N.
- **Remark:** Use for justification of saying or asserting, not primarily proof that N is true.
- **Example:** (NS-Justify (text "I will not eat this dish.") (text "I am a vegetarian."))

### Motivation
- **N:** N is an action to be performed by R.
- **N/S:** S increases R's desire or willingness to perform N.
- **Example:** (SN-Motivation (text "The city would benefit enormously.") (text "Apply to host the games!"))

### Non-volitional Cause
- **N/S:** S is a non-deliberate cause of the situation in N.
- **Example:** (SN-Non-volitional Cause (text "Because the temperature fell,") (text "the road froze."))

### Non-volitional Result
- **N/S:** S is a non-deliberate result caused by N.
- **Example:** (NS-Non-volitional Result (text "The temperature fell.") (text "The road therefore froze."))

### Otherwise
- **S:** S presents an unrealized situation.
- **N/S:** N depends on the non-realization of S.
- **Example:** (NS-Otherwise (text "Leave now.") (text "Otherwise you will miss the train."))

### Purpose
- **N:** N is an activity.
- **S:** S is the intended situation or goal realized through N.
- **Example:** (SN-Purpose (text "To protect the troops,") (text "officials withheld the route."))

### Restatement
- **N/S:** S repeats N in different wording and at roughly equal scope.
- **Example:** (NS-Restatement (text "The councillors received every detail,") (text "in other words, they were fully briefed."))

### Solutionhood
- **S:** S presents a problem, question, request, or need.
- **N/S:** N provides or contributes to a solution.

### Summary

- **N/S:** S gives a shorter condensation of N.

### Volitional Cause
- **N/S:** S causes or motivates an intentional action in N.

### Volitional Result
- **N/S:** S is an intentional action or response resulting from N.

### Preparation
- **N/S:** S precedes N and makes R more ready, interested, or able to interpret N.

### Contrast

### Joint

### List

- **Example:** (NN-List (text "Cook dinner.") (text "Clean the bathroom."))

### Sequence
- **N:** The nuclei describe events or steps in temporal or procedural order.
- **Example:** (NN-Sequence (text "The teacher entered.") (text "Five minutes later she announced a test."))
