**TL;DR**: The text is a financial news summary with multiple independent segments. The overall structure is a **Joint** of macro-segments (stock market activity, economic indicators, corporate news, market data). Internally, many relations are **Contrast** (via "but" signals), **Attribution** (reporting sources), **Elaboration** (explaining consequences), and **Same-unit** (handling interrupted clauses). The full tree is built bottom-up, referring to fragments by index.

### Step-by-Step Discourse Tree Construction

#### 1. Preliminary Analysis: The Text's Macro-Structure
The text is a classic financial news roundup consisting of loosely related items. There is no single overarching narrative. The macro-structure is thus a **Joint** of several multi-EDU segments, each covering a distinct topic:
- Segment A: Program trading curbs and reactions (1–9)
- Segment B: Economic indicators (10–16)
- Segment C: Corporate legal and M&A news (17–30)
- Segment D: Auto/electronics stakes and analyst views (31–40)

*Self-Correction during assembly*: The initial flat macro-Joint needs to properly group sub-segments to maintain a binary/n-ary tree. The above structure groups A-F under one root `NN-Joint`. Within each letter segment, binary relations combine the EDUs. The tree respects the ordering, uses `NN-Joint` for the macro-structure (each segment is a different type of information but serves the overall purpose of a financial roundup), and applies semantically specific relations whenever possible (e.g., `Reason` over `Cause`, `Attribute` for reporting verbs, `Purpose` for infinitive clauses). The `Same-unit` relation was avoided except where truly discontinuous, and `Conjunction` was used for explicit "and" combinations.
